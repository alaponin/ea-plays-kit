# Published national EA frameworks and coordination instruments

**Use this list to start a search. Do not use it in place of a search.** Verify each entry
when you run the skill: URLs move, one framework supersedes another, and governments change
the structure of their agencies. Search also for comparators that are *not* on this list. A
set from this list alone is a set of the countries that were well documented in 2026.

## Known frameworks to check

| Country | What to look for | What it is not |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Kenya** | The Government Enterprise Architecture; the standards of the ICT Authority and their legal basis | Evidence of enforcement. Check whether the standards bind a procurement |
| **Ghana** | The Government Enterprise Architecture; the arrangements that coordinate the digital services | — |
| **South Africa** | The Government-Wide Enterprise Architecture (GWEA); the mandate of SITA | The age of GWEA matters. Check what superseded it |
| **Rwanda** | The framework of RISA, and its mandate over the ministries | — |
| **India** | IndEA and the versions after it. The country is federal, so adoption by the states is very different from state to state | A national mandate. The question is adoption |
| **Estonia** | X-Road governance, the once-only obligation in law, and the interoperability framework | A model for scale. The country has 1.3m people |
| **Nigeria** | The frameworks of NITDA, and their status in a federal system | — |
| **Liberia** | The technical-clearance mechanism that the August 2026 runs found. It is a gate for spending or approval | — |
| **Sierra Leone, Malawi, The Gambia** | The digital strategies, and the bodies that they create to coordinate. Check whether the mandate binds | Mature EA frameworks. These countries are at an earlier stage, which is frequently the *more* useful comparison |
| **Nepal** | The Digital Nepal framework. It is a useful lower-middle-income comparator that is not in Africa | — |

## Contested cases to know

Search for the current state of each case. These cases move. A card that gives a ruling from
2021 and not the appeal is wrong.

- **Kenya, Huduma Namba** — the litigation about the national ID programme. The tip uses
  this example. Look for the judgments, the findings of the data-protection impact
  assessment, and the programme that followed.
- Programmes that a **data-protection regulator** or an **audit office** stopped. Search the
  decisions of the regulator and the reports of the auditor-general directly. These are Tier
  1 sources, and a general search almost never finds them.
- **Frameworks that a government published and never applied.** This is the most common
  failure, and the failure that people report least. The evidence has this shape: a framework
  document from year *n*, and procurement or project documents from year *n+3* that do not
  reference it. That difference is a legitimate finding, and you can source it.

## What a card must not do

A card must not give the **plan** of a country as its achievement. Each national digital
strategy describes an architecture. Almost none of them describe an architecture that
exists. The *what it built* row needs evidence of operation: a member list, a transaction
volume, an audit, or an evaluation. A chapter heading is not evidence.
