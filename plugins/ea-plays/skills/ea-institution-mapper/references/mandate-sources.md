# Finding the establishing instrument

## Where instruments are published

| Instrument type | Look at | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Act of parliament | The national **gazette** or law portal; the parliament's own site | The gazette is the authority. A ministry's copy may be an earlier version. |
| Statutory instrument / regulation | The gazette; the parent ministry's legal page | Often where the real mandate detail lives. |
| Presidential or cabinet decree | The presidency's or cabinet office's site; the gazette | Common for digital agencies created between legislative sessions. |
| Establishment order for an agency | The parent ministry; the agency's own "about" or "legal" page | An agency page describing its mandate is **not** the instrument — find the citation it refers to. |
| A body created by a strategy | — there is none | This is the finding. Record "no establishing instrument found; mandate asserted in [strategy, year]" and mark the body's authority as *coordinating, not binding*. |

Where no national law portal exists, try in order: the parliament's bill tracker; the
ministry of justice; the UN or FAO legal databases (FAOLEX carries far more than
agriculture); a regional body's legal repository.

## Telling a current act from a superseded one

Search results favour the original act, not its amendments. Before quoting a clause:

1. Search the act's title plus "amendment" and plus "repeal".
2. Check the gazette index for the years since enactment.
3. Look at whether the body's own site cites a later instrument than the one you found.
4. If a consolidated version exists on the law portal, prefer it and say it is consolidated.

Where you cannot establish currency, quote the clause, name the act and year, and mark it
⚠ *currency not established* rather than presenting it as in force.

## Reading a mandate clause

The clause that matters is usually the **functions** or **objects** section, not the
preamble. Three things to extract:

- **What it may do** — the functions listed.
- **Whether its decisions bind others.** "Shall coordinate" and "shall direct" are
  different mandates and the whole EA governance question turns on which one it is. Quote
  the verb.
- **What it may not do** — many instruments explicitly reserve functions to another body,
  and that reservation is what a RACI has to respect.

## Donor project documents as a source

World Bank PADs and ICRs, EU and FCDO evaluations describe institutional arrangements in
more detail than most official sites, because the arrangement had to be described to get
the money. They are **Tier 2**: cite them, then chase the instrument they cite.

Read a PAD as **intended** arrangements and an ICR as **actual** ones. A PAD's
"the Authority will be established" is not evidence the Authority exists.

## The name trap

Sources will give you the name of the current post-holder in almost every case, because
that is what press coverage and "about" pages carry. The register records **the post**.
Drop the name as you write the row, not in a review pass — a name that reaches the draft
reaches the RACI, and the RACI reaches a briefing. This happened in the August 2026 test
runs and is the reason the rule is a rule.
