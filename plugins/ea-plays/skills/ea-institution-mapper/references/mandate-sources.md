# How to find the establishing instrument

## Where governments publish instruments

| Type of instrument | Look at | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| An act of parliament | The national **gazette** or the law portal; the site of the parliament | The gazette is the authority. The copy on the site of a ministry can be an earlier version. |
| A statutory instrument or a regulation | The gazette; the legal page of the parent ministry | The detail of the mandate is frequently here. |
| A decree of the president or the cabinet | The site of the presidency or the cabinet office; the gazette | This is common for a digital agency that a government creates between sessions of the parliament. |
| An establishment order for an agency | The parent ministry; the "about" page or the "legal" page of the agency | A page of an agency that describes its mandate is **not** the instrument. Find the citation that the page refers to. |
| A body that a strategy creates | — there is no instrument | This is the finding. Record "no establishing instrument found; mandate asserted in [strategy, year]". Then record the authority of the body as *coordinating, not binding*. |

If the country has no national law portal, try these in order: the bill tracker of the
parliament; the ministry of justice; the legal databases of the UN and the FAO, because
FAOLEX contains much more than agriculture; and the legal repository of a regional body.

## How to see the difference between a current act and one that is superseded

A search gives the original act more frequently than its amendments. Do these four steps
before you quote a clause:

1. Search the title of the act with the word "amendment", and then with the word "repeal".
2. Look in the gazette index for each year after the act.
3. Look at the site of the body. It can cite a later instrument than the one that you found.
4. If the law portal has a consolidated version, use it, and say that it is consolidated.

If you cannot establish that the text is current, quote the clause and give the name and the
year of the act. Then mark it ⚠ *currency not established*. Do not give it as in force.

## How to read a mandate clause

The clause that matters is usually the section for the **functions** or the **objects**. It
is not the preamble. Take these three items from it:

- **What the body may do** — the functions in the list.
- **Whether the decisions of the body bind other bodies.** "Shall coordinate" and "shall
  direct" are two different mandates. The full question of EA governance depends on which
  one the clause uses. Quote the verb.
- **What the body may not do.** Many instruments keep some functions for another body. A
  RACI must respect that reservation.

## Donor project documents as a source

World Bank PADs and ICRs, and EU and FCDO evaluations, describe institutional arrangements
in more detail than most official sites. The people who wrote them had to describe the
arrangement to get the money. These documents are **Tier 2**. Cite them, then find the
instrument that they cite.

A PAD gives the arrangements that a project **intends**. An ICR gives the arrangements that
the project **made**. "The Authority will be established", in a PAD, is not evidence that
the Authority exists.

## The trap of the name

Almost every source gives you the name of the person who holds the post now. Press coverage
and "about" pages carry that name. The register records **the post**. Remove the name while
you write the row. Do not wait for a review. A name that gets into the draft gets into the
RACI, and the RACI gets into a briefing. This happened in the August 2026 test runs. This is
why the rule is a rule.
