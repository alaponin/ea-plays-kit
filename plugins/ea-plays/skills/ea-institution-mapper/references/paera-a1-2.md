# The body taxonomy — seven types, and the five that the videos teach

## Read this first

This file **helps you to map the types. It does not replace the specification.** PAERA v1.0
Annex A1.2 is the authority for the names of the types and for their definitions. Read Annex
A1.2 again at **paera.govstack.global**, and use its words, before you use a classification
in a document that the bodies concerned will see. Cite the annex. Do not cite this file.

The kit does not copy the text of the annex. It is not confirmed that the licence permits a
verbatim copy, so the kit references the annex by its section and reads the published version
again when the skill runs. If the licence is confirmed, this file becomes a verbatim copy,
and that change is a minor version bump that names the PAERA version.

## The five teaching types

The videos of plays 2.4, 2.5, 4.1 and 4.8 use these five types. `bdat-assessor` also carries
them. They are correct as far as they go. They are a deliberate simplification for an
explanation of four minutes.

| Type | Primary role | Expected data domains | Typical risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Policy Unit** | Sets policy and owns the rules. Does not operate services at scale | Policy instruments, standards, funding allocations | The capability moves into regulation or delivery with no mandate |
| **Regulatory Agency** | Licenses, supervises and enforces. Holds registers and decisions | Register of the regulated bodies, licences, decisions, appeals | It builds its own infrastructure for delivery instead of using the shared platforms |
| **Service-Delivery Authority** | Operates services to citizens at scale | Case files, transactions, outcomes, queues | Duplicate registries, lock-in to a vendor, bespoke build |
| **State Registry** | The authoritative single source for one domain | One canonical domain: person, learner, business or land | The ministry that hosts it treats it as its own asset, not as a shared resource |
| **Shared Platform** | Identity, payments and data exchange, used by many bodies | Few. It gives infrastructure, not content | Fragmentation, if each sector builds its own instead of using the platform |

*Source: PAERA v1.0 §4.6 and Annex A1.2, as `bdat-assessor` carries them.*

## What the teaching subset does not contain

Annex A1.2 has **seven** types. The subset of five does not show three concepts:
**Horizontal System**, **Natural Digital Environment** and **Public Ecosystem**. Read the
annex for their definitions, and for how the seven types divide the space. Do not assume
that the five types map one to one onto five of the seven types. They do not.

This is an outline. Confirm it against the annex.

- **Horizontal System** — a system that gives one common function to many bodies across the
  sectors: document management, workflow, messaging, human resources, or finance. It is
  different from a Shared Platform. Many bodies *operate their own instance* of a horizontal
  system, or *use it as a service*. It is not a foundational rail such as identity or
  payments. Countries build these systems several times and rarely see that they did.
- **Natural Digital Environment** — services that a government delivers inside the digital
  contexts that citizens already use, and not in a channel that the government owns. PAERA
  carries the same idea as principle #7 (§5.2).
- **Public Ecosystem** — the arrangement in which actors outside the government deliver a
  part of a public service. These actors are banks, mobile operators, schools, clinics and
  civil society. It classifies an *arrangement* and not one body. This is why it has no
  equivalent in the five types.

## How to write a classification

```
Progressa Digital Government Authority — Shared Platform [teaching type: Shared Platform]
  · basis: coordinating but not binding mandate; a unit under the Ministry of ICT (§1, §4)
  · confidence: confirmed (A0 §1)
  · hybrid: also Policy Unit — the 2021 e-Government Interoperability Framework gives it a
    standards role that is published, not applied (§7)
```

Obey these three rules:

1. **Write the full type first, and the teaching type in brackets.** A learner who watched
   the video must be able to compare the output with the course. A learner who did not watch
   it must get the answer of the specification.
2. **The confidence is about the evidence. It is not about how certain you feel.**
   *Confirmed* means that you read the clause. *Inferred* means that you read the behaviour.
   There is no third state.
3. **Flag each hybrid body. Never resolve a hybrid in silence.** A ministry that sets policy
   and operates a national system is both types. The tension between the two is usually the
   reason that the sector is fragmented. To write one type deletes the finding.

## A classification is a claim about a mandate

You must be able to trace each type to a clause in an instrument. If you cannot, mark the
classification *inferred*. A body that you classify from its website is a body that you
classify from its aspirations.
