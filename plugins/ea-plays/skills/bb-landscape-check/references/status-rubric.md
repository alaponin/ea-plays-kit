# What each status requires

A status is a claim about the world. This rubric gives what must be true before you write
the status. If your evidence supports two statuses, write the lower one.

| Status | Required evidence | Not sufficient |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **live** | A statement by the operator, a report by a regulator or an auditor, or a completion report by a donor, that says the system is in production. You also need a named operator. You also need a minimum of one of these: a figure for coverage or volume, a published member list, or a documented onboarding procedure. | A strategy that names it. A press release about a launch with nothing after it. A case study by a vendor. A slide from a conference. An entry in a deployment map with no date. |
| **pilot** | The same evidence as *live*, but the source says pilot, phase 1, or limited members. You can also use it when the member list has only the few bodies that started the block. | A programme that intends to start a pilot. |
| **planned** | A commitment that has funding and a date, in a strategy, a budget line, or an approved donor project. | An aspiration in a speech. A sector plan that asks for something and names no funder. |
| **none** | You searched each source in `block-sources.md` for this block and found only aspiration. Say which sources you searched. | You did not look. |
| **unclear** | The sources do not agree, or the most recent statement is more than two years old, or you cannot identify the operator. | — use this status instead of a guess. |

## The trap that this rubric closes

A World Bank appraisal document, a PAD, describes what a project **will** build. The
completion report, an ICR, describes what the project **did** build. To read a PAD as
evidence of a live system is the most common way that a *planned* block becomes a *live*
block in a register. This error puts a platform that does not exist into a target
architecture.

The same trap has three other forms:

- a national strategy that lists a data-exchange layer among its pillars;
- an entry in a GovStack map or a DPI map that records the *engagement* of a country and not
  its deployment;
- a "digital services" page of a ministry that describes the service that the ministry
  intends to give.

## Availability is a separate judgement

A block can be **live** and still not be **available to a new sector**. Record the two
judgements separately. A block is available when all four of these are true:

1. There is a documented onboarding procedure that a new member can follow.
2. There is a legal basis for the data flows of a new member, and not only for the flows of
   the members that started the block.
3. The cost is known, and a ministry can pay it.
4. The operator accepts members now. The operator has not paused for a second phase.

If you do not know one of the four, write *unknown* in the column. The gate decision that
depends on the column is then not ready.
