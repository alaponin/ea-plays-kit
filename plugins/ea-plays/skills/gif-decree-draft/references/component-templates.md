# The five components and the six articles — their shape

A decree is five parts, each with its own reader. Build them in parts, so that the
Ministry of Justice can perfect the Articles without reopening the case in the Memorandum.
The shapes below are what each play's prompt asks for. Keep them. Do not add a component.

## The five components and their readers

| Component | Reader | Length | Play |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explanatory Memorandum | the minister | 1–2 pages | 2.3 |
| Preamble | the lawyer at the Ministry of Justice | a scaffold of recitals to the enacting clause | 2.3 |
| Draft Articles Package | the lawyer | one article per run of 2.4 | 2.4 |
| Cover Note | the Ministry of Justice | three short paragraphs | 2.5 |
| Two-Track Regulatory Memo | the data-protection authority | sections (a) to (e) | 2.5 |

## B9 — the outline (2.2)

For each of the five components: the sub-points it will carry, and the inputs it draws on
(B4 §MANDATE, B4 §SCOPE, B8 row n, A0 §10). The Articles sub-list names the six articles
below in this order. The outline is a drafting plan, not legal content; it carries no
clause text.

## B10 — the Memorandum and the Preamble (2.3)

**Memorandum**, four headed sections: the problem (from B1 and A0 §8–§9, with the
citizen's repeated burden in one concrete sentence); what the decree does (authorise,
mandate connection, protect data, name the authority — four sentences); why now (the
programmes in A0 §2 that wait on it); the expected benefit (from B3, in words, with the
figure marked ⚠ if it is an estimate). Every factual claim is evidenced or flagged.

**Preamble**: recitals in the order *the authority under which the decree is made* → *the
policy it implements* → *the consultation held* → the enacting clause. Each authority is a
citation from the register or `[confirm: <the kind of authority needed — e.g. the
constitutional provision on subordinate legislation>]`.

## B11 — one operative article (2.4)

The article, numbered as the outline numbers it, in the structure of its model. Then the
coverage table (catalogue exchange → clause). Then the `[confirm]` list and the flagged
terms. Six articles exist:

1. **Scope and definitions** — which exchanges and which bodies; the defined terms.
2. **Mandatory connection** — the designated bodies *shall* connect; the timetable; the
   authority that designates further bodies.
3. **Once-only obligation** — a body shall not ask a person for data that the state holds
   and can lawfully fetch; the authentic source; the record of the fetch; the person's
   right to see what was fetched.
4. **Data protection and consent** — the lawful basis per exchange; purpose limitation;
   minimisation; the safeguards; the relation to the data-protection act (defer where it
   speaks).
5. **Principles** — adopted by reference from the EIF; one line each.
6. **Operating authority's powers and duties, dispute and enforcement** — the powers
   (admit, remove, catalogue, rules, monitor, report), the split from the regulator
   (B14), and the ladder below.

### The enforcement ladder

Enforcement is a ladder, not a hammer. Each step is proportionate, or the first sanction
applied is the one a court strikes down.

| Step | Measure | Who decides | `[confirm]` |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | written warning with a deadline | the operating authority | — |
| 2 | administrative fine | the regulator | whether the decree can carry a fine, or only primary legislation |
| 3 | restriction of access to the framework's services | the operating authority, on the regulator's decision | the appeal route |
| 4 | suspension of membership | the regulator | notice period; effect on the member's citizens |
| 5 | escalation to the Council of Ministers | the regulator | the instrument of escalation |

## B12 — the Cover Note and the Two-Track Memo (2.5)

**Cover Note**, three paragraphs: what the package contains (the five components and the
open-items annex of `[confirm]`s); the actions requested — review and perfect the
Articles, confirm the Preamble authorities, advise on the route to enactment (executive
decree or primary legislation, with `[confirm: whether an enabling law exists]`); the
timeline and the cost of delay (the programmes in A0 §2 that wait).

**Two-Track Memo**, five sections: (a) the two instruments and the status of each; (b) the
points of intersection — definitions, lawful basis, consent and data-subject rights,
cross-border transfer, the data-protection officer, overlapping sanctions; (c) for each
point, whether the decree **defers**, **fills a gap**, or **could conflict**, and how a
conflict is resolved, with the section of the act that you read; (d) the coordination
mechanisms — joint subordinate regulation, a memorandum of understanding between the
operating authority and the DPA, joint enforcement; (e) the risk if the data-protection
law changes during drafting, and the forward-compatible choices that mitigate it.

The memo's value depends on the real text of the act. A memo that coordinates with a law as
the model imagines it is worse than none.
