# Finding published exemplars

Two or three per document type, for **illustration only**. Never merge exemplar text into
the draft — the point is to show the learner how a real government wrote it, not to supply
wording whose context you do not know.

## Where they live

| Document | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| **Board ToR / architecture review board mandate** | National digital agencies' governance pages; published EA framework documents, which often annex the board's ToR; cabinet office circulars |
| **Digital spend control** | The finance ministry or treasury — spend controls are usually a treasury instrument, not an ICT one. The UK, Canadian and Australian published spend-control policies are the most documented; search for African and lower-middle-income equivalents first. |
| **Repository / architecture standards** | The ICT authority's standards register; the national interoperability framework, which usually names the repository |
| **Gate checklist** | Published project-approval or technical-clearance procedures. Liberia's technical clearance surfaced in the August 2026 runs as one of these. |
| **Scorecard / metrics** | Annual reports of digital agencies; auditor-general reports, which often state what *should* have been measured |
| **Risk register** | Rarely published as such. Auditor-general and evaluation reports are the substitute — they describe the fade modes after the fact, which is exactly the evidence a register needs. |

## Telling an adopted instrument from a draft

Four checks before citing an exemplar:

1. **Is it on the government's own domain?** A consultancy's or donor's copy may be a draft
   that was never adopted.
2. **Does anything cite it?** An adopted ToR is referenced by later procurement documents,
   board minutes or annual reports. A framework nobody cites was published and shelved.
3. **Is there a date and a version?** Undated governance documents are usually drafts.
4. **Does the body it creates show any activity?** Meeting records, decisions, an annual
   report. A board with a ToR and no minutes is the failure mode, not the exemplar.

A document that fails these checks is still worth citing — **as an example of the
characteristic failure**, which is often more instructive than a success. Say which you are
showing.

## What to write per exemplar

One line: what it does well, and the one thing worth taking. Not a summary.

> **[Country] Architecture Review Board ToR (2022)** — binding scope is written as six
> decision types each citing the procurement regulation that enforces it, rather than as
> areas of interest. Take the structure of §2.
> `<URL>` · T1 · checked 2026-09-05

Run `cite-or-discard` on every exemplar URL. An exemplar the learner cannot open is worse
than none — they will assume the rest of the draft is as checkable as its citations.
