# How to find published exemplars

Find two or three exemplars for each document type. Use them **for illustration only**.
Never put the text of an exemplar into the draft. The purpose is to show the learner how a
government wrote the document. The purpose is not to give you words whose context you do not
know.

## Where the exemplars are

| Document | Look at |
| --- | --- |
| **Board ToR, or the mandate of an architecture review board** | The governance pages of the national digital agencies; the published EA framework documents, which frequently have the ToR of the board in an annex; the circulars of the cabinet office |
| **Digital spend control** | The finance ministry or the treasury. A spend control is usually an instrument of the treasury and not of the ICT body. The published spend-control policies of the United Kingdom, Canada and Australia have the most documentation. Search first for the equivalents in Africa and in lower-middle-income countries |
| **Repository and architecture standards** | The register of standards of the ICT authority; the national interoperability framework, which usually names the repository |
| **Gate checklist** | The published procedures for project approval or technical clearance. The August 2026 runs found the technical clearance of Liberia, which is one of these |
| **Scorecard and metrics** | The annual reports of the digital agencies; the reports of the auditor-general, which frequently say what the agency *should* have measured |
| **Risk register** | Governments rarely publish one. Use the reports of the auditor-general and the evaluations instead. They describe the fade modes after the event, and this is the evidence that a register needs |

## How to see the difference between an adopted instrument and a draft

Do these four checks before you cite an exemplar:

1. **Is the document on a domain of the government?** A copy on the site of a consultancy or
   a donor can be a draft that nobody adopted.
2. **Does another document cite it?** Later procurement documents, minutes of a board, or
   annual reports cite a ToR that a government adopted. Nobody cites a framework that a
   government published and then put away.
3. **Does it have a date and a version?** A governance document with no date is usually a
   draft.
4. **Does the body that it creates do anything?** Look for records of meetings, decisions,
   or an annual report. A board with a ToR and no minutes is the failure mode. It is not the
   exemplar.

A document that fails these checks is still worth a citation, **as an example of the
characteristic failure**. That is frequently more instructive than a success. Say which one
you show.

## What to write for each exemplar

Write one line: what the document does well, and the one thing to take from it. Do not write
a summary.

> **[Country] Architecture Review Board ToR (2022)** — the binding scope is six decision
> types. Each type cites the procurement regulation that enforces it. It is not a list of
> areas of interest. Take the structure of §2.
> `<URL>` · T1 · checked 2026-09-05

Run `cite-or-discard` on the URL of each exemplar. An exemplar that the learner cannot open
is worse than no exemplar. The learner will then assume that the rest of the draft is as
weak as its citations.
