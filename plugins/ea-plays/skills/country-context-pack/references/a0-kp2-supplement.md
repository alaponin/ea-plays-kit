# A0 §8–§10 — the KP2 supplement

KP2, the Government Interoperability Framework course, reads three things that the seven
sections of A0 do not hold. The KP2 Play 0 supplement adds them as §8, §9 and §10. They
extend A0; they do not replace it. Build them with the same rules as §1–§7: public sources
only, a URL and a data year on every claim, posts not names, text in the chat.

Run them when the learner names KP2, an interoperability framework, a decree, a data
exchange layer, or asks for §8, §9 or §10. Write the three sections after §7 with the same
provenance header, or as a separate artefact `A0 §8–§10 — KP2 supplement` if §1–§7 already
exist.

## §8 Current exchange approach — feeds 1.1

Four headings, three to four paragraphs. Each institution, system and document with its
year; a source URL per substantive claim.

1. Whether an interoperability platform or data-exchange layer **exists** — name,
   technology, year, operator, number of connected bodies — or is planned. The NIIS X-Road
   world map and the operator's own pages are the first sources; a donor project document
   the second.
2. How interoperability is **required** today: procurement clauses, an interoperability
   framework document, a law, or nothing. The procurement portal's standard bidding
   documents; the digital agency's published framework; the legal register.
3. The two or three best-known **point-to-point** integrations between public bodies, and
   who maintains them. Audit reports, ICT strategy annexes, donor ICRs.
4. Any published **assessment of duplication**, or of citizens re-submitting the same data.
   Supreme audit institution reports; a sector plan's situation analysis; a World Bank or
   UNDP diagnostic.

## §9 Integration map — feeds 1.2, 1.3, 1.5

A table of the 6–10 exchanges of data between public bodies that the sector's services
depend on or would benefit from, then three observations on where the same data is asked
for more than once.

`| # | Providing body · registry | Consuming body · service | Entity exchanged | How it happens today (live integration / file transfer / citizen carries a certificate / not at all) | Citizen re-supplies? | Documented (URL) or inferred |`

Mark each row *documented* with its source or *inferred from the services involved*. An
inferred row is a hypothesis for the learner to test with the two bodies, and the map says
so. Sources: the service catalogue of the e-government portal; the sector plan; the
registries' own pages; a citizen-facing "documents required" list on a service page is the
best evidence that a citizen re-supplies.

## §10 Data-protection law and DPA — feeds 2.1, 4.8

Four numbered parts, as text. Cite the instrument and a public source URL for every claim;
where a document cannot be found, say so — an honest gap is a finding.

1. The **data-protection law** in force: title, year, the article or section that governs
   public-sector sharing, and whether a lawful basis for once-only sharing exists.
2. The **data-protection authority** or equivalent: name, year established, powers, whether
   it operates (staff, enforcement actions, guidance published).
3. Any law, decree or regulation that already **mandates or restricts** exchange between
   named bodies — identity, civil registration, tax, the sector's own act.
4. The **gaps a lawyer would name** before an interoperability decree could be enacted.

Sources, in order: the national gazette and law portal (T1); the UNCTAD Global Cyberlaw
Tracker and DLA Piper's *Data Protection Laws of the World* (T2, to find what exists,
then go to the instrument); the DPA's own site; World Bank ID4D country diagnostics. This
section overlaps with the Legal register that `ea-legal-context` builds; §10 is the first
pass, the register is the full one. Do not build the register here.
