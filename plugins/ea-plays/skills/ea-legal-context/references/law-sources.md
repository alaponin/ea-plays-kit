# Where national law is published, and what each source can prove

## Tier 1 — the authority

| Source | Proves |
| --- | --- |
| The **national gazette** | The instrument as published, and — via the index — its amendments and commencement orders |
| The **national law portal** (where one exists) | Often a consolidated text; say so when you cite one |
| The **parliament's** site and bill tracker | The status of anything not yet enacted |
| The **ministry of justice** | Where there is no portal; often the fallback repository |
| The **owning ministry's** legal page | The instrument, but possibly an earlier version |

Where none of these exist or are reachable: **FAOLEX** (the FAO legal database, which
carries environment, land, food, water and much general legislation for many countries),
the regional economic community's legal repository, and the UN treaty and national
implementing legislation collections.

## Tier 2 — the trackers

| Tracker | Covers | Cannot prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **UNCTAD Global Cyberlaw Tracker** | Data protection, e-transactions, cybercrime, online consumer protection — presence and broad status, most countries | The current text; the amendment history; commencement |
| **DLA Piper, Data Protection Laws of the World** | A readable summary of the data-protection regime per country | Anything since its last update; sector-specific rules |
| **World Bank ID4D country diagnostics** | The identity and civil-registration legal framework, assessed | Current status — diagnostics lag by years |
| **OGP action plans** | Access-to-information and open-data commitments, and whether they were met | Whether a commitment became law |

Every tracker entry is a lead. Chase it to the instrument, cite the instrument, and note
the tracker as *found via*.

## Establishing currency — the four checks

1. Search the act's title plus **"amendment"**, and plus **"repeal"**.
2. Check the **gazette index** for every year since enactment.
3. See whether the **owning body's own site** cites a later instrument than the one you
   found.
4. Look for a **consolidated version** on the law portal; prefer it, and say it is
   consolidated and as at which date.

If all four are inconclusive, the entry is ⚠ *currency not established*. Do not round that
up to *in force*.

## Citing an instrument

Cite as the jurisdiction cites it: the short title, the year, and the chapter or act number
where the country uses one — *Data Protection Act 2023 (Act No. 12 of 2023), §14*. Give the
section for the clause you rely on, not just the act. A ToR that cites an act without a
section has not been checked.

## What is never a source for law

A news article describing a law. A consultancy's summary. A vendor's compliance page. A
blog post explaining "what the new act means". Any of these may be how you *find* the act;
none of them is the act. See `source-tiers.md`.
