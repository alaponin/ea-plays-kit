# Where governments publish law, and what each source can prove

## Tier 1 — the authority

| Source | Proves |
| --- | --- |
| The **national gazette** | The instrument as published. Through the index it also proves the amendments and the commencement orders |
| The **national law portal**, if the country has one | Frequently a consolidated text. Say so when you cite it |
| The site and the bill tracker of the **parliament** | The status of an instrument that is not yet an act |
| The **ministry of justice** | Use it when there is no portal. It is frequently the repository of last resort |
| The legal page of the **owning ministry** | The instrument, but possibly an earlier version |

If none of these exists, or you cannot open them, use **FAOLEX**, which is the legal
database of the FAO. For many countries it carries the law on environment, land, food and
water, and much general legislation. Also use the legal repository of the regional economic
community, and the UN collections of treaties and of national implementing legislation.

## Tier 2 — the trackers

| Tracker | Covers | Cannot prove |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **UNCTAD Global Cyberlaw Tracker** | Data protection, e-transactions, cybercrime and online consumer protection. It gives the presence and the broad status for most countries | The current text; the history of amendments; the commencement |
| **DLA Piper, Data Protection Laws of the World** | A summary of the data-protection regime of each country, in readable form | Anything after its last update; the rules of a sector |
| **World Bank ID4D country diagnostics** | An assessment of the legal framework for identity and civil registration | The current status. A diagnostic is some years behind |
| **OGP action plans** | The commitments on access to information and open data, and whether the government met them | Whether a commitment became law |

Each entry in a tracker is a lead. Follow it to the instrument. Cite the instrument. Record
the tracker as *found via*.

## Four checks to establish that a text is current

1. Search the title of the act with the word **"amendment"**, and then with the word
   **"repeal"**.
2. Look in the **gazette index** for each year after the act.
3. Look at the **site of the owning body**. It can cite a later instrument than the one that
   you found.
4. Look for a **consolidated version** on the law portal. Use it, say that it is
   consolidated, and give the date to which it applies.

If the four checks give no answer, mark the entry ⚠ *currency not established*. Do not
change that to *in force*.

## How to cite an instrument

Cite the instrument as the jurisdiction cites it. Give the short title, the year, and the
chapter or act number if the country uses one: *Data Protection Act 2023 (Act No. 12 of
2023), §14*. Give the section of the clause that you use. Do not give only the act. Nobody
checked a ToR that cites an act with no section.

## What is never a source for law

A news article about a law. A summary by a consultancy. The compliance page of a vendor. A
blog post with the title "what the new act means". You can *find* an act with one of these.
None of them is the act. See `source-tiers.md`.
