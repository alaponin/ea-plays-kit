# The instruments to look for

Search for each instrument in this list. Record the instruments that exist. Also record the
absence of the instruments that do not exist. An absence is a constraint on the design, and
it is the constraint that people miss most frequently.

| Instrument | Why it applies to an architecture | What its absence means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Data protection act** | It gives the regime of legal bases for each data flow between bodies. It gives the special rules for the data of minors and for health data. It says whether processing outside the country is lawful | There is no framework of lawful bases. You must then justify each sharing arrangement with its own instrument, and consent becomes the default. Consent does not work at scale |
| **The regulator that the act creates** | It decides whether a body exists to approve, to register, or to object. It decides whether you need a DPIA | An act with no regulator that operates is an obligation with no counterparty. Record the act as in force **and** record that the capability to enforce it is absent |
| **E-government or e-transactions act** | It decides whether an electronic record and an electronic signature have legal effect, and whether a service can be digital first | Paper stays the authoritative record. A digital service still needs a paper trail, and this is a cost in each wave of the roadmap |
| **Public procurement act and e-procurement rules** | They decide what a body can procure, for how long, and whether an ICT commitment can cross financial years | An annual cycle of awards makes an operating contract for a shared platform weak. The sourcing matrix must record this |
| **The act that establishes the ICT agency or the digital ministry** | It decides whether the decisions of that body **bind** other bodies, or only coordinate them. This is the most important fact for EA governance | A body that coordinates and does not bind cannot hold a gate. The Board ToR must find its authority in another place, such as a spending gate or a cabinet directive |
| **E-government decree or cabinet directive** | Where there is no act, this is frequently the true source of the authority of a digital agency | — |
| **Civil registration act** | It says which record is the legal anchor of identity, and at what age. It says whether a body can rely on a birth record | The identity architecture has no legal anchor. A learner registry cannot resolve a record to a person |
| **Identity or national ID act** | It gives the legal status of the ID, the uses that it permits, and whether another body can require it | To use the ID as a key between sectors can be unlawful, also when it is technically possible |
| **Statistics act** | It says whether a body can reuse administrative data for statistics, and whether the statistics office has a claim on the data in a registry | A registry design that ignores this act is reopened later |
| **Access-to-information act** | It says what a body must publish, and what a repository can hold in the open | It changes the policy for openness of the EA repository (3.1, 3.3) |
| **The sector act** | It gives the obligations of the sector for data and for reporting, and it says which body owns which register | The ownership of the registry of the sector is not settled. You then have a duplicate-registry finding with no legal remedy |
| **Cybersecurity act and the mandate of the CISO** | They give the security obligations of a shared platform, and they say who accredits it | There is no path to accredit a new national system |
| **The strategies in force** | They are not law. They are the political commitment that a Board ToR cites | — record them, and record whether a body approved them or they are drafts |

## Two differences that matter more than they look

**In force is not the same as enacted but not commenced.** A parliament can pass an act, and
a minister must then make a commencement order. If that order never comes, the act is not in
force. In each search result the two look the same. Check the provision for commencement,
and look in the gazette for the order.

**Coordinating is not the same as binding.** Quote the verb from the functions clause.
*"Shall coordinate"*, *"shall advise"* and *"may recommend"* do not bind another ministry.
*"Shall direct"*, *"shall approve"* and *"no ministry shall procure without"* do bind it.
The governance argument of Module 1 and the design of the Board in Module 3 both depend on
the verb in the instrument. This is the line that is most worth a direct quotation.

## Where the question of the learner usually lands

Most questions from a learner have this form: "can we share X with Y?". The answer is
usually in four instruments at the same time: the data protection act, for the basis; the
sector act, for the ownership of the register; the civil registration act or the ID act, for
the identity; and the e-transactions act, for the legal weight of the record. Answer with all
four instruments, or say which ones you could not find.
