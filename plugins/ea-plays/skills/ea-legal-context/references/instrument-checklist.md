# The instruments to look for

Search for all of these. Record the ones that exist, and record the absence of the ones
that do not — an absence is a design constraint, and it is the one most often missed.

| Instrument | Why it bears on an architecture | What its absence implies |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Data protection act** | The legal basis regime for every data flow between bodies; special rules for minors' and health data; whether cross-border processing is allowed | No lawful basis framework: every sharing arrangement has to be justified instrument by instrument, and consent becomes the default, which does not scale |
| **The regulator it creates** | Whether there is anyone to approve, register or object; whether a DPIA is required | An act with no operative regulator is a compliance obligation with no counterparty — record the act as in force *and* the enforcement capability as absent |
| **E-government / e-transactions act** | Whether an electronic record and an electronic signature have legal effect; whether digital-first delivery is lawful | Paper remains legally authoritative; a digital service still needs a paper trail, which is a cost in every wave of the roadmap |
| **Public procurement act and e-procurement rules** | What may be procured, over what period, and whether multi-year ICT commitments are possible | Annual award cycles make shared-platform operating contracts fragile; the sourcing matrix must account for it |
| **Act establishing the ICT agency or digital ministry** | Whether its decisions **bind** other bodies or merely coordinate — the single most important fact for EA governance | A coordinating body with no binding mandate cannot hold a gate; the Board ToR must find its authority elsewhere (a spending gate, a cabinet directive) |
| **E-government decree or cabinet directive** | Often the *actual* source of a digital agency's authority where no act exists | — |
| **Civil registration act** | Who the legal identity anchor is, and at what age; whether a birth record can be relied on | An identity architecture with no legal anchor; a learner registry cannot resolve to a person |
| **Identity / national ID act** | The ID's legal status, permitted uses, and whether other bodies may require it | Using the ID as a cross-sector key may be unlawful even where it is technically available |
| **Statistics act** | Whether administrative data may be reused for statistics, and whether the statistics office has a claim on registry data | Registry designs that ignore it get reopened later |
| **Access-to-information act** | What must be published; what a repository may and may not hold openly | Affects the EA repository's own openness policy (3.1, 3.3) |
| **The sector act** | The sector's own data and reporting obligations, and which body owns which register | The sector's registry ownership is unsettled — a duplicate-registry finding with no legal remedy |
| **Cybersecurity act / CISO mandate** | Security obligations on a shared platform; who accredits | No accreditation path for a new national system |
| **Strategies in force** | Not law, but the political commitment a Board ToR cites | — record them, and record whether they are approved or draft |

## Two distinctions that matter more than they look

**In force vs enacted-but-not-commenced.** An act passed with commencement left to a
minister's order that never came is not in force. It reads identically in every search
result. Check the commencement provision and the gazette for the order.

**Coordinating vs binding.** Quote the verb from the functions clause. *"Shall coordinate"*,
*"shall advise"*, *"may recommend"* — none of these bind another ministry. *"Shall direct"*,
*"shall approve"*, *"no ministry shall procure without"* — these do. The whole of Module 1's
governance argument and Module 3's Board design turn on which verb is in the instrument, and
this is the single line most worth quoting verbatim.

## Where the sector question usually lands

Most learner questions ("can we share X with Y?") resolve to four instruments at once: the
data protection act (basis), the sector act (whose register it is), the civil registration
or ID act (whose identity it is), and the e-transactions act (whether the record counts).
Answer with all four, or say which you could not find.
