## What this skill does

This skill drafts the two documents in KP2 that a Strategist signs or presents. Play 1.4
makes **B4, the Strategic Foundation Document**: MANDATE, SCOPE, PRINCIPLES, SUCCESS
MEASURES, roughly two pages, formal. The home-page play makes **B38, the country
storyboard**: one page, the journey from fragmentation to the first live once-only service
across the five modules, mapped to the phased plan, for a minister or a funder.

Both are drafting plays, and both create obligations or expectations. The bare play
defends with a safeguard — negotiate the scope, keep the timeline honest. This skill adds
the **sources**: the mandate cites the instrument that gives the authority (the legal
register, A0 §7); the principles are adopted from the EIF and PAERA by citation, with one
implication each, not drafted fresh; every figure comes from an earlier artefact or is
marked ⚠; and the calendar is the method's, stated once and not softened.

## Inputs

| Play | Makes | Needs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.4 | B4 Strategic Foundation Document | B1 (the procured-vs-planned diagnostic); B3 (the highest-value once-only exchange); A0 §7 (the instruments); **a 1–3 paragraph brief** — who mandates the framework, the first-wave agencies, the first exchanges, the constraints |
| home | B38 Country storyboard | B5 (the catalogue — the first exchange and its agencies); B28 (the phased plan and its calendar); **the constraints** |

Also useful: the Legal register; B6 for the first-wave agencies; A0 §8–§9 for the
fragmentation paragraph.

Ask at most three questions, together: who mandates (the post and the instrument, if the
learner knows it); the first exchange if B3 or B5 does not settle it; for the storyboard,
whether the audience is a minister or a funder, since the closing line differs. Record the
answers in the artefact.

## Procedure

1. **Ground the mandate in an instrument.** Search the legal register and A0 §7 for the
   authority under which the framework exists: a digital-government act, an establishing
   act of the digital agency, a cabinet decision, a national strategy in force. Write the
   mandate section around it, cited. If the authority is a draft or a coordinating mandate
   that does not bind (A0 §1 often says), write that — the framework's mandate is then a
   thing the decree (Module 2) must create, and the document says so rather than implying
   an authority that does not exist.

2. **Scope from the artefacts.** The first-wave agencies from the brief and B6; the first
   exchanges from B3 (1.4) or B5 (storyboard); what is explicitly out of scope for now.
   Every agency named is a post-holding body, not a person.

3. **Adopt the principles by citation.** Whole-of-government, once-only, reuse-first,
   cross-border readiness — from PAERA §5.2 and the EIF's underlying principles
   (`references/principle-sources.md`). One line to state each, one line for its
   implication for a design decision in this country (the implication is yours; the
   principle is theirs). Do not add a principle the sources do not carry.

4. **Success measures from the numbers you have.** Three to five, measurable, for 18
   months, at least one a once-only measure (the citizen asked once for the first
   exchange; documents no longer requested). A baseline comes from B1, B3, A0 §8–§9; a
   figure with no source is ⚠. Never invent a baseline to make a target look precise.

5. **For the storyboard, keep the calendar honest.** The method's calendar is in
   `references/honest-calendar.md`: the foundation — decree, governance, architecture,
   standards — a year to eighteen months, longer in calendar time; then the build phases —
   the platform and its first pilot members in the first six months, the first
   cross-ministry once-only exchange in the second, first-wave sectors by month eighteen;
   the first visible milestone two to three years from day one; national coverage four to
   six years. Map the six stations of the storyboard to the five modules and to B28's
   phases. If B28 gives a different calendar, use B28's and say where it departs from the
   method's and why. Close with the one line on why it works — the framework gives business
   and IT a shared language to build together — and with the procurement line: built, not
   bought, and then enforced through every tender.

6. **Run `cite-or-discard`** on the instrument and principle citations.

## Output contract

Write the provenance header first (`references/provenance-header.md`). Then:

For **1.4 (B4)**: the document in four headed sections — MANDATE (with the instrument
cited), SCOPE (in and out, first wave), PRINCIPLES (four, each with its source and its
implication), SUCCESS MEASURES (3–5, with baselines and ⚠ where unsourced); a signature
block by post; then the list of every ⚠ and every point the named agencies must agree.

For **home (B38)**: the one-page storyboard in six stations — (1) the starting
fragmentation, (2) foundation, (3) the decree, (4) governance, (5) architecture, standards,
semantic map, contracts, (6) stand-up, onboarding, the live once-only exchange — each
mapped to its module and to the phase and month range of the calendar; the closing line;
then the list of every figure and its source.

Then the **Inputs supplied by the learner** block and the safeguard. Text in the chat,
never a file or a slide. Posts, not names — *the Minister of ICT*, never the minister.
Write no analysis before the header. See `references/output-contract.md`.

## Safeguard handed back

**A foundation document is a starting draft to negotiate, not a final charter. A storyboard
is a plan to inspire and align, not a guarantee.**

- The scope and mandate sections create real obligations. Agree them with each named
  agency and clear them with counsel before signature. A mandate cited from a draft
  instrument is a mandate the decree must still create.
- The credibility of the storyboard rests on the first once-only service being achievable
  on the calendar stated. Do not let the first phase promise more than B28 costs.
- The success measures with ⚠ need a baseline before they go in front of a minister.
- If the calendar in the storyboard is shorter than the method's, the person who
  shortened it owns the explanation.
