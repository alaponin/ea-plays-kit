# The honest calendar

The method's calendar, as KP2 states it. Tell it to the minister on day one. A storyboard
that shortens it is the storyboard that is not believed when the launch enthusiasm fades
and the minister changes.

## The foundation, then the build

| Stage | What is built | Calendar |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foundation | the decree, the governance, the architecture, the standards | a year to eighteen months of work — longer in calendar time, because legislative and budget cycles do not hurry |
| Build phase 1 | the platform and its first pilot members | the first six months of the build |
| Build phase 2 | the first cross-ministry once-only exchanges | the second six months |
| Build phase 3 | the first-wave sectors | by month eighteen of the build |
| Build phase 4 and after | national coverage | a four-to-six-year programme |

The first real milestone — a running platform with a handful of member services — lands
two to three years from the day the programme starts. Not years of silent build and then
a big-bang launch; not six months either. A visible, gated win each phase.

| Station | What happens | Module | Phase |

## The lines that must not be softened

- The foundation comes before the build. A country cannot skip the decree and "do the
  legal part later"; the bus carries nothing without a lawful basis.
- The first cross-ministry once-only exchange lands in the **second** build phase, not the
  first.
- National coverage is years. Say the number.
- Interoperability is built, not bought — and once the framework exists, procurement is
  how it is enforced: the reference architecture becomes the tender specification.
- The closing line: the framework gives business and IT a shared language to build
  together. Without it, the conversation the build requires never happens, and the
  programme stalls.
