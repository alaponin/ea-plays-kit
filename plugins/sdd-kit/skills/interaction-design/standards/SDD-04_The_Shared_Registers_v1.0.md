> **What this standard promises, and what it cannot.** It does not promise that two analysts given one record will describe it the same way. No published discipline promises that. It promises that **two analysts who have both finished will have answered the same questions about the same record**, so that every difference between their answers can be pointed at and argued about one question at a time. A record is finished under this standard when every line of the form at §5 carries an answer, or carries a written reason for having none. Nothing else counts as finished and nothing more is required.

## 1. What this standard is for, and the gap it fills

### 1.1 The gap

`SDD-03` §1 names four things it does not cover and says *"nothing here restates them"*. **All four are this standard's subject**: **identity and uniqueness**, **ownership**, **states**, and — new in version 0.2 — **shared lists and reference data**. Until this edition the fourth was sent to a document that was never written, and was therefore excluded by both standards and claimed by neither. It was a hole and not a boundary, and it is closed here.

The method volume in force says the same thing from the other side. `SDD-01` §4 divides the shared groundwork in two. **Until its version 3.2 of 1 September 2026 it said of those halves: _"The first half has a standard behind it. The second half has none, and that is the largest single gap in this method."_ That sentence is gone, because this document is what removed the gap it named, and it is quoted here from the superseded version 3.1 rather than from the edition in force.** `SDD-01` §17 named the missing standard for *"the residue the entity model standard explicitly leaves out: who owns each fact, what states a record moves through, and what makes a record unique"* — and named three further registers with no standard at all: the catalogue of events, the catalogue of computations, and the assignment of shared code lists. **Version 0.2 of this document takes all three.** They are here rather than in documents of their own by the owner's ruling of 1 September 2026 (`rulings/2026-09-01-the-extension-reversal.yaml`, XR-1), which reversed an earlier ruling of the same day that would have written two new standards. The case against is recorded in that file.

**Until this document exists, those three things are governed by a sentence saying they are governed elsewhere.** That is the gap. Six measured costs of it are recorded in the outline this standard is drawn from, and two more were found while writing it: two records shipped on one instance carrying the identifier `DC-000001`, and a newly created record saved with a blank status. Both were bought at production.

**Version 0.3 adds a fourth catalogue, the catalogue of settings**, for the values the administration may change by its own act — a threshold, an interval, a switch. The method volume has required every such figure to carry a marked default since it was written (`SDD-01` §14 rule 6), and no standard said where a setting lives or what its entry carries; a check of 5 September 2026 found one of the fourteen things an entry owes stated as a rule. It lands here because the method volume's own account of the fifth thing is *everything else a use case draws on but does not own*; because this is the standard that covers the part of the shared groundwork the entity model sets aside; because the requirements standard, disclaiming the subject, reaches for the word *register* (`SDD-02` §2.3); and because the four rules about any register at §4D govern it without being restated. **The case against is this document's length**, which §9.3 calls its largest defect. The one alternative home — a family of the entity model standard beside the register of business rules — was tested against the fourteen things and not taken: on shape the two homes are even, and the alternative is the longer document, so length chooses neither. A reading by the owner that the register of business rules and the settings are one subject would move it; the remedy for length remains the split that the ruling of 1 September 2026 declined, and that ruling is not reopened here.

### 1.2 What this standard governs, in two sentences

For every record the entity model names, this standard governs **what makes two rows the same thing, who writes each of the record's facts and whether each is stored or worked out afresh, and the states the record moves through and the moves between them.** Beside those it governs four catalogues the same records depend on: **what the module announces has happened, what it works out rather than stores, which lists a coded value is allowed to point at, and which values the administration may change by its own act — and what each of those values owes before it may be read.** It governs the **registers** that hold those answers — what each carries, who owns it, how it is regenerated, and what a program reads from it — and not the drawing, not the model, and not the storage.

### 1.3 The mark every rule carries, and what a mark is not

Every rule at §4 carries two things, and they answer different questions.

**The mark** says what kind of support the rule has:

| Mark | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **supported** | A clause of a standard in force states the obligation. The clause is named on the rule |
| **measured** | This estate's practice evidences it, **with a count made on the material, on a named file, at a named moment**. A field filled by hand forty-one times is evidence; a field somebody thinks would be useful is not |
| **derived** | No clause states it and no count shows it, but it follows from one or both by reasoning the rule sets out |
| **original** | Nothing but this standard's judgement. **A reviewer should test these hardest** |

**The bar** says whether the rule has passed the house method's test, which is a different and harder question: *a recognised discipline states it, and the same pattern recurs in at least two independent fields drawn from a list fixed before any searching began.* The fields were fixed and the searching was done; both are in `SWEEP_gw01.md`. Each rule carries one of four values: **cleared** — two or more independent fields state it · **one field** — a discipline outside this estate states it once · **not cleared** — it was tested against the fields and no field states it · **not tested** — no field was searched for it.

**A rule can be supported and not have cleared the bar** — because the standard supporting it is one of this estate's own, and this estate is one field. That combination is the commonest position in this document and it is stated on every rule rather than averaged away.

### 1.4 What this standard does not cover, and where instead

Each boundary in one sentence.

- **Which records exist, what one row means, what identifies it, and how records relate.** → `SDD-03`, EM-1 to EM-33 — and, since its version 0.3, EM-45 to EM-51 for whose a record or a list is, who issues it and where the issuer publishes it, which that standard states first and this one cites. *EM answers "what is the key"; this standard answers "what do we do when two rows might be the same thing", and neither question can be answered from the other's answer.*
- **Attributes, and the strength with which one is bound to a list.** → EM-17, EM-19 and EM-20 for the attribute; **§4G of this standard** for the list's own entry, and since version 0.3 for the entry of a named selection of a list, which EM-19 admits since the entity model's version 0.4. *The entity model tells the analyst what to write on the attribute; §4G tells her what she is allowed to point it at.* **Until version 0.2 the second half of that sentence pointed at a document that did not exist.**
- **A value only the platform sees — a connection string, a thread count, what a ready-made component accepts.** → `SDD-08` ARC-9 to ARC-12 and ARC-14. *The catalogue of settings at §4H carries every value whose effect the business can see; IOS-41 draws the line, and both sides of it are configuration in ordinary English.*
- **Where a setting shows — in a use case's header and at its step, on a screen as a value or as an effect.** → the standards that govern the use case and the screen, `SDD-06` and `SDD-07`. *This standard says what a setting's entry carries and that every reader cites it by name; it does not say how a reader shows it.*
- **How the records are stored.** → EM-25 and the database. *This standard never names a table, an index or a key the system makes up.*
- **The glossary, and the register of business rules.** → `SDD-03` EM-34 to EM-44. *A word the system keeps no rows of, and a constraint that holds whatever flow invokes it, both belong beside the entity model and not here.*
- **The kernel's identity resolution service — matching, survivorship, the golden record, the cross-reference, and the correction of a published value.** → `MDM-001` §5.7, §7.2.2, §7.2.3, §8.9, §11.6, the master-data standard of the programme this standard was first written for, which is not public. *That standard governs how the platform resolves two records to one party; this standard governs what each module's register must declare so that the question can be asked at all.* **This boundary is new and is the largest correction the stocktake made to the design this standard was drawn from.**
- **Anything about a boundary crossing beyond who writes the fact and what the system does when the writer is unreachable.** → the architecture specification, named as missing by `SDD-01` §17 when it carried the code `S2C-04`; the document now exists as `SDD-08`, in force.
- **What a screen may do with any of it.** → `UX-01`, family 4.11.
- **How much detail the register carries, and which of the entity model's three levels it is written for.** → **Neither is answered here.** Both are inherited unanswered from `SDD-03` §8.1; see §8.

## 2. The words, defined once

| Word | What it means here |
|---|---|
| **A record** | A kind of thing the system keeps rows of, as the entity model names it. Not a row, and not a table |
| **A row** | One instance of a record |
| **A fact** | One thing a record says about itself. The unit ownership is decided at — **not the record**, because a record can be wholly this system's and still hold facts it does not write |
| **The grain** | The answer to *what does one row represent*, written as one sentence beginning *one row per* |
| **The same thing** | Two rows are the same thing when they stand for one thing in the world, whether or not their keys agree |
| **A match** | A proposal that two rows are the same thing, carrying a **grade**: **certain** · **probable** · **possible** |
| **Adjudication** | A person deciding whether a proposed match is a real one |
| **The writer** | The one party that may change a fact. Every other holder of it is a **reader** |
| **Stored** | A fact kept as it was written |
| **Worked out afresh** | A fact computed from other facts each time it is asked for, naming the computation that does it |
| **A copy** | A fact this system does not write, kept here anyway, carrying the act that froze it and the moment it was frozen |
| **A state** | One of a closed set of conditions a record can be in, exactly one at a time |
| **A move** | A declared passage from one state to another, with what fires it and who may |
| **A terminal state** | A state with no declared move out of it, other than a move the standard names as the way back |
| **The register** | The document that carries these answers for every record, generated from a named source |
| **A finding** | A gap, written down, with somebody's name against it and a date. The only permitted response to something this standard cannot answer |
| **The issuer** | The body whose act makes a value a member of a governed list, as `SDD-03` EM-48 defines it: a legislature, a standards body, an office. Never the software that stores or serves the list |
| **The steward** | The person or unit in this administration who answers for a list *here* — who is asked when a member is missing, a binding is in doubt or an edition moves — as distinct from the issuer |
| **A named selection** | A named subset of a governed list, bound to as if it were the list, with an entry of its own. It selects members; it never adds one |
| **A setting** | A value inside a rule, a trigger, a computation or a screen that the administration may change by its own act while the statement it sits in stays true. Not a fact of law, which only a legislature changes; not a design decision, which only the builder changes; not a member of a list, which is a meaning and not a value |
| **The entry** of a setting | The one place its name, kind, default, authority, class of change, path, ownership and readers are written. **The catalogue of settings** is the register of those entries, one for each module |
| **A class of change** | A named group of settings whose changes are made on one occasion, by one role, seconded by one role where one must, and tried in one way before they take effect. The classes are the specification's to name and there is at least one |
| **A switch** | A setting with two values, which turns a capability on or off |
| **A placeholder** | A default that is a guess, marked as one, with an owner and the occasion on which it will be replaced by an answer |

## 3. The principles

Five. Each one is the reason a family of rules exists, and each is stated so that a rule can be traced back to it.

**P1 · A question is answered where it can be checked, and once.** A fact recorded in two documents will come to say two things. Where another standard governs a point, this one cites it and adds nothing.

**P2 · The unit of ownership is the fact, not the record.** EM-2 answers ownership per record in three answers. That is a different question from this one, and answering it does not answer this one.

**P3 · A record's identity survives what happens to it.** The row changes, the name changes, the states change, the parts merge and split; the identity is the thing that persists, and the register's job is to say how.

**P4 · Nothing is lost quietly.** A merge keeps both identifiers; a correction is a new version; a deletion happens only under a named authority and is itself recorded; a count that will not reproduce is reported rather than fixed.

**P5 · A rule a program cannot read is a rule a person enforces, and the standard says which it is.** No result may be reported from a check that has not been built. This standard prints its checks as what will be refused once they exist, and prints no result of any kind.

## 4. The rules

**Forty-seven rules in eight families.** Nineteen of them are version 0.1's and thirteen version 0.2's, and **not one was renumbered**: a reader holding a citation to any of IOS-1 to IOS-32 finds the same rule under the same number. Fifteen are new in version 0.3: three join family G, and twelve are family H, the catalogue of settings. Seven of the thirty-two gained a clause in version 0.3 — IOS-31's is a citation of the rule it builds on — and each says so on its face; one, IOS-28, was reworded to cite the entity model for what that standard states first since 2 September, and one word of IOS-32 changed, *owner* to *issuer*, because two rules of this family used *owner* for the issuer and a third for the register's owner, and the entity model has since given the issuer its name. Families A to D answer what a record is, who writes it and how it moves; families E to H are the four catalogues that sit beside it.

> ⚠ **The warning on IOS-6, and it is the most important sentence in this section.** IOS-6 is this standard's load-bearing rule and it is the one the sweep attacked hardest. **It was refuted as a universal.** A named published discipline instructs the opposite in as many words — *"Don't Expect a Single Source of Truth"* — and multi-master replication is a legitimate design in which several processes write one record and conflicts are resolved rather than prevented. **No source outside this estate states one-writer as an absolute obligation**; the strongest is *strive towards*. The only document that states it absolutely is this platform's own, and it immediately qualifies itself: the boundary *"is logical rather than physical. It survives only if the design declares it and review enforces it."* **A reviewer who believes this rule is received practice everywhere should read that sentence and this one instead.**

> **The carve-out on IOS-9, and why it is in the rule rather than in a note.** The rule as the design proposed it said nothing is deleted. A lawful erasure request can compel physical deletion, and the exemption that would save an archive — *"processing is necessary for archiving purposes in the public interest"* — **does not cover an operational register of debts.** A rule that made this estate unlawful on contact with a valid request would have been printed as received practice. It is not, and the carve-out is the correction.

> **What IOS-10 does not govern, said because a reader will otherwise think it refuted.** It governs **a record's** states. It does not govern **work**. CMMN exists because *"adaptive processes are unstructured"* and *"Not all work that happens during a case is actually modeled in the Case Plan"*, and there is a published position that state is better derived from an event log than declared as a machine. Both are about the process. ADR-001 already draws the line — *"Processes orchestrate; lifecycles govern."* — and IOS-10 is written on the lifecycle side of it. **The rule was widened during drafting so that a derived state satisfies it**, because as first proposed it would have refused a legitimate design.

### H · The catalogue of settings — 12 rules

**New in version 0.3.** A setting is a value inside a rule, a trigger, a computation or a screen that the administration may change by its own act while the statement stays true: *a debt above the threshold becomes a case* is a rule, and the threshold is a setting. Whether a figure is a setting at all — or a fact of law only the legislature changes, a decision only the builder changes, or a member of a list, which is a meaning — is a reviewer's question at §6.2 and not a rule. **Twelve rules, for the fourteen things an entry owes**, written as the pairs two of them are: the entry and its readers are one two-sided relation, and a change's dating and its record are one thing. IOS-15 to IOS-18 govern this catalogue as they govern the other three, and are cited rather than repeated. **Every setting names its class of change and a module defines its classes once; the method does not fix the classes, their number or their axis** — one module's two zones are one answer, another's three are another, and a module with one class has answered.

Every rule below was tested against the one real catalogue there is — the eight settings of one module's specification, as its use case model carried them on 3 September 2026. ## 5. The form the analyst fills

Seven parts. Part 1 is filled once for each record and Part 2 once for the register as a whole; Parts 3, 4 and 5 are new in version 0.2 and are filled once for each event, each computation and each governed list — and, since version 0.3, Part 5 once more for each named selection of a list; Parts 6 and 7 are new in version 0.3 and are filled once for each setting and once for each class of change. **Every line names the rule that requires it and the evidence that the line is worth asking for** — because a form line that nobody can say where it came from is how a form grows. Three lines of Part 5 named a rule in version 0.2 that did not state what the line asked — the list's identifier, the edition in use, and how a consumed list degrades — and three obligations were counted as governed on the strength of them when they were not; version 0.3 gives each its rule text, and the *Rule* column of every line now names a rule that states it.

### 6.2 What only a person can judge

No program will ever decide these, and a review that skips them has checked that the boxes are full rather than that the answers are right.

- Whether the **grain sentence** is the grain the business would recognise, or the grain the table happens to have.
- Whether the **duplicate rule** is *the* rule, as opposed to *a* rule that catches the cases somebody thought of.
- Whether the threshold on the key-absent path is set where a false match costs more than a missed one, or the other way round, and whether anybody decided that on purpose.
- Whether the named **writer** is the real writer, or the module that noticed first.
- Whether a fact called **stored** should be worked out afresh, and whether a stored derivation's recompute trigger is one that will actually fire.
- Whether a **state set** is the business's lifecycle or one project's stages.
- Whether an **inherited spine** was inherited or copied.
- Whether a **finding** recorded against another document is a real gap or this analyst declining to decide.
- Whether an **event** is one happening or two, in the case the rule does not reach: a correction issued after the fact, which may be a second event or an amendment of the first.
- Whether a **computation** produces the figure the business means by that word, as opposed to a defensible figure with the same name.
- Whether a **list** is really shared, or is one this system happens to have given to somebody else once.
- Whether a binding is at the strength somebody chose, or at the strength the first attribute happened to use.
- Whether a **selection** is the module's to define, or a slice somebody else already publishes under a name.
- Whether a figure inside a rule, a trigger or a screen is a **setting** at all — could it lawfully be otherwise tomorrow by this administration's act, would the statement stay true, and is what changes a value and not a meaning — or a fact of law, a decision, or a member of a list.
- Whether a **class of change** was chosen for a setting or defaulted to the one nearest to hand; and whether the classes a module defined are its own or one product's administration screens under other names.
- Whether a switch's **barred** rests on legal advice received or on a reading of the provision by whoever filled the entry.
- Whether a setting's **default** is an answer with a ground or a guess that was not marked as one.
- Whether the register is **finished**, for which **no published test exists**, and the answer is the entity model standard's: every line answered or carrying a written reason.

## 7. The ways people get this work wrong

Named so that a reviewer can look for them. Each has happened, most of them in this estate.

1. **Two rows, one identifier.** Two records shipped on one instance carrying `DC-000001`, because the identifier generator guaranteed the *shape* of the identifier and not its uniqueness, and the guard that would have caught it was bound to a read-only field and never fired. **A declared uniqueness that nothing enforces is a sentence, not a rule.**
2. **A record created with no state.** A new application saved with a blank status, because the lifecycle declared an initial state and nothing applied it on create.
3. **The unique constraint mistaken for the duplicate rule.** A key constraint prevents exact duplicates and does nothing about the same person entered twice slightly differently — which is what manual entry, pre-registration and a mistyped key produce every day.
4. **A merge done by hand.** Two rows reconciled by an officer with a screen and no rule, destructively, with no record of which identifier survived.
5. **The split limb forgotten.** Merge is published and split is answered by nobody, and the page reads as complete.
6. **Ownership answered at the record and thought to be answered.** EM-2 answers ownership per record. A record wholly this system's under EM-2 can still hold three facts it does not write.
7. **A stored total that drifts.** A number that could be computed and is also stored, with nothing saying which is authoritative and no trigger keeping the stored one honest.
8. **A copy that is nobody's.** A consumed value copied in for convenience, with no source, no as-of, and no way to tell it has gone stale.
9. **A correction that rewrites history.** A figure changed in place, so that last year's decision can no longer be reproduced and the reproduction is not even known to be impossible.
10. **A state entered and never exited.** Found by the first row that enters it, in production.
11. **A Hold button.** A user-triggered move into a state the rules are supposed to determine.
12. **A state that is really a computation**, stored as a field and computed elsewhere.
13. **A spine copied instead of inherited**, so one record has two state machines and neither is wrong.
14. **A register hand-edited to make a description pass**, silently reverted by the next regeneration.
15. **A count typed once and carried until somebody checked.** *"45 / 37"* was published on 10 August 2026 and on 12 August 2026 could not be reproduced from the register's own tables, which recounted **40 published · 20 consumed**. Two days, not three weeks — the interval is short and the lesson is not that it was long.
16. **A count re-measured and quietly corrected**, which asks to be trusted on precisely the thing it just got wrong.
17. **A column nobody has ever filled**, surviving in the form because nobody looks at whether anybody uses it.
18. **A check written and reported as run.** A written check reads like a working one, and the difference is invisible to everybody except the person who tried to run it.
19. **An event named for the handler.** *Send reminder* rather than *instalment missed*, so that the record of what happened cannot outlive the thing that reacted to it.
20. **The same happening counted twice**, because a retry was recorded as a second event and nothing said it was not.
21. **Two systems announcing one change**, and a consumer with no way to tell which announcement is the real one.
22. **A recipe named and never written.** Five facts on one register marked *worked out afresh*, each naming a computation, with nowhere for any of the names to resolve — which is the case this standard cited from the day it was written and could not close until version 0.2.
23. **Two implementations of one figure**, each defensible, discovered in a meeting about which one is the number.
24. **A local table of somebody else's codes**, three versions behind, that nobody is watching because nobody remembers making it.
25. **A code deleted rather than retired**, silently rewriting what older rows mean.
26. **A required binding to a list nobody maintains** — a rule the system enforces on behalf of nobody.
27. **A list minted locally to carry a slice of a shared one**, because nothing let the module say it was bound to a selection.
28. **A member added locally to a list somebody else governs**, and an executive act admitted that the Code does not know.
29. **A boundary and a code list bound under one identifier.** Twelve use case descriptions on one programme bound the band boundaries under the band list's name, because nothing said a name can carry a code and a value.
30. **A threshold in two documents with two values**, and nothing saying which one the system applied.
31. **A switch carried as a column of the list it is keyed by**, so that the list's row states what EM-47 says it states nothing of.
32. **Seven settings with no class, and one setting assigning approvers per class** — the class was the specification's own idea and nothing made it name one per setting.
33. **A choice offering a value nothing implements.** *Generate the instrument*, offered for every act, real for one.
34. **A boundary moved with live cases under it**, re-categorised on the day, and nobody having previewed which.
35. **A guess with no mark on it.** A hundred and four placeholders among a hundred and forty-three values, indistinguishable from the answers beside them.

## 8. What this standard does not answer

Named here rather than discovered at review.

1. **How much detail the register carries** — whether a record is described with three facts or thirty. Inherited unanswered from `SDD-03` §8.1. The analyst records the depth she worked at and keeps to it.
2. **Which of the entity model's three levels the register is written for.** The same section leaves it open for the entity model; a register that hangs off the entity model cannot answer it first.
3. ~~**What happens to rows already carrying a value since retired from its list.**~~ **ANSWERED in version 0.2, at IOS-31.** It was recorded here as jointly this standard's and another document's, and answerable by neither alone. Both halves are now in this standard, so it is answerable, and it is answered as one of three declared outcomes rather than left to the analyst. `SDD-03` §8.2 question 6 asks it from the other side and is closed by the same rule. **OQ-5, closed.**
4. ~~**In what shape the register publishes its machine-readable sets.**~~ **ANSWERED in version 0.4, at IOS-15.** It was recorded here as the single largest hole in this document: IOS-15 required a named source and did not say what a parser reads, and every check at §6.1 was blocked on it. The shape is now a setting, `registers.published_set_shape`, owned by the module's architect, which carries no value in this standard and whose value each module's catalogue of settings carries. **OQ-12, closed.**
5. **Whether one state machine may be shared by two records with different writers.** IOS-14 carries the boundary that would not go into a sentence. **F-6.**
6. **Whether the register is per module or per estate.** In practice it is per module; the reference-data register is not; nothing here settles it.
7. **Any threshold on any count.** None was found and inventing one would mean inventing a number nobody has counted.
9. **Whether a correction issued after an event is a second event or an amendment of the first.** IOS-21 says what makes two events the same event and does not reach a correction, which is the case that will arise first. **New: OQ-14.**
10. **In what shape a computation's recipe is written down.** IOS-25 requires the inputs, the rules and the moment; it does not say whether the recipe itself is prose, an expression, or a reference to code. Naming a shape would mean choosing a language, and no field was found choosing one for a register. **New: OQ-15.**
11. **Whether a list may be shared by agreement without an owner having been named.** IOS-32 forbids the strongest binding to a list with no owner and does not forbid the list. Whether an unowned shared list may exist at all is the method owner's, not this standard's. **New: OQ-16.**
8. **Where the boundary runs between this standard's duplicate rule and the kernel's identity resolution service** in the case the kernel does not cover — a duplicate of a record that is not a party. `MDM-001` §5.7 is about party identity; a duplicate case of a module is nobody's. **New: OQ-13.**
12. **How a setting consumed from another module's configuration is consumed, and what this module does when that configuration is unreachable.** IOS-42 names the module and the crossing, and IOS-8's closed set of patterns is written for a fact of a record. Whether the same set serves a value, and whether a consumed setting may be frozen under IOS-9, is not settled. **New in version 0.3: OQ-17.**
13. **Whether the classes of change should be fixed by the method after all.** IOS-40 requires that they exist and are defined once, and fixes neither their number nor their axis; two independent fields state that changes are classed and each class carries its own procedure, and none states which classes. One module's two zones and another's three both satisfy the rule. Whether that is enough is what use will show. **New in version 0.3: OQ-18.**
14. **What the catalogue of settings owes a second module.** The twelve rules of family H were tested against one real catalogue of eight settings and against no other; the two questions that catch nothing on those eight — whose the setting is, and what other setting constrains it — are kept for the cases the owner's design and the programme's register would have failed, and may be the first to fall. **New in version 0.3: OQ-19.**

### 9.2 What the sweep refuted

**Seven of the twenty refutation attempts produced a refutation, and between them they changed four rules.** Three of the four were refuted in the form the design proposed them and are rewritten rather than removed: **IOS-9** (never delete → not deleted except under a named legal authority, and the deletion recorded), **IOS-6** (one writer everywhere → authority within one administration's transactional boundary), and **IOS-10** (a declared state machine → a state set declared where a program can read it, which a derived state satisfies). The fourth, **IOS-19**, the design did not have at all: it was written from the sweep and then narrowed by the sweep's second attack on it, from *a person adjudicates every match* to *a person adjudicates every match not graded certain*. **A sweep that refuted nothing would have been run to confirm rather than to test.**

### 9.3 The departures from the design this standard was drawn from

1. **Nineteen rules, not eighteen.** IOS-19 is new. It exists because the sweep found a rule the design did not have and the platform's own MDM standard already states — that a match below the grade *certain* is adjudicated by a person. **The size consequence is real and is not hidden: the design already put this standard at the ceiling of the estate's measured band, and a nineteenth rule pushes against it.**
2. **This document is longer than the estate's standards are.** The design measured the three standards this method instantiates at 7,302, 7,623 and 8,801 words. Converted differently in this pass they come out at 7,630, 8,228 and 9,197 — **neither conversion is re-derived here and the two disagree**, which is itself worth knowing before anybody sets a threshold on it. Version 0.1 reported itself at 10,023 words, counted on the finished file at 15:27 UTC on 31 August 2026, and said that was already above every figure in both sets — nine per cent above the largest figure in either (9,197) and fourteen per cent above the largest the design measured (8,801). **Version 0.2 runs 13,854 words**, counted at 13:04 UTC on 1 September 2026 on the finished file, taking every paragraph and every table cell including the first page. **That count does not reproduce version 0.1's, and the discrepancy is reported rather than corrected in place, which is what IOS-17 requires of this document about itself.** Re-measuring the superseded file with today's counter gives **9,462 words**, not 10,023. The two figures were taken by different counters and neither is wrong about what it counted; neither is reconciled here. **Like for like, on the counter used today, this edition is forty-six per cent longer than the one it replaces**, and half as long again as the largest figure either conversion produced for the three standards this method instantiates. The design predicted the problem at eighteen rules and called it *"the first thing its drafting run must watch"*; thirty-two rules and three subjects the design did not have make it much worse, and nothing was taken out to pay for them. **This is now the largest single defect in this document.** A reviewer is entitled to ask what comes out, and there are two obvious candidates: §4D's four register rules, which are here by sequence rather than by subject and are the answer the earlier edition already named, and — larger — the three catalogues themselves, whose removal is the split ruling XR-1 declined. **Neither is proposed here, because deciding what comes out of a standard on the day it doubled is how a document loses the part somebody needed.**
4. **Three subjects the design did not have at all.** The design was written for one document about identity, ownership and state. Version 0.2 carries three catalogues besides, by the owner's ruling of 1 September 2026 rather than by anything the design or the sweep found. **This is the largest departure of the four and the one a reviewer should weigh first**, because it is a decision about where a subject lives and not a finding about what is true. What is said for it is in the ruling; what is said against it is in the ruling too, and the short form is that this document's title had to change to stay true.
3. **A boundary against `MDM-001` that the design does not have.** The stocktake found that the platform's approved master-data authority already governs party matching, survivorship, the golden record, the cross-reference and backdated correction, normatively. §1.4 draws the line. Without it this standard would have restated another standard's rules, which P1 forbids.
5. **A fourth catalogue, and the length again.** Version 0.3 adds fifteen rules and two parts of the form to the document whose length its own item 2 above calls its largest defect. **Version 0.3 runs 22,327 words**, counted at 10:40 Tallinn on 6 September 2026 on the finished file by the counter used for version 0.2, taking every paragraph and every table cell including the first page — sixty-one per cent longer than version 0.2's 13,854, on the same counter, and against a set whose largest other member is the entity model standard at rather more than sixteen thousand by a different converter. The placement was tested before this edition was written, against the fourteen things an entry owes and against the one alternative home, and §1.1 says why it was kept; the remedy for length remains the split that ruling XR-1 declined, and a fourth catalogue makes that question harder to leave, not easier. It is recorded here as the cost it is, and nothing was taken out to pay for it, for the reason item 2 gives.

### 9.5 What a verification pass over this document found

Recorded because a document that reports only its conclusions is asking to be trusted on the part it did not check.

An adversarial pass was run over this draft and its three companions against every source cited inside this estate, re-deriving every count from the file it was made on. **It found nineteen defects and all nineteen are corrected in this version.** The ones worth a reader's attention:

- **Four counts did not reproduce.** The ADR register's status split; the number of ADR files carrying a subject term (39 → 59); *"39 occurrences of MUST"* in `MDM-001`, which is 39 **lines** and 55 occurrences; and the grain total, which is **45** and not 44 — the fourth form, an inline grain in a table cell, had been counted in the body and dropped from the heading.
- **An interval was wrong by an order of magnitude.** IOS-17's evidence was described as two figures carried for *three weeks*. The figures were published on 10 August 2026 and found not to reproduce on 12 August 2026. **Two days.** The rule is unchanged and its illustration was overstated.
- **The stocktake had not walked what it said it had walked.** It reported totals for seventeen roots as totals of the tree; the tree has **thirty-one**, and 98 further `.docx` and fourteen further directories were outside the first walk. The second walk is the one now recorded, and the first is recorded as having been wrong.
- **The bar table mixed two axes.** It listed a *mark* (original) as though it were a *bar* value, and put two rules that had been tested and failed into a row labelled *not tested*. §9.1 now tallies the two separately and says why they do not line up.
- **IOS-8's bar was overstated** as *one field* when both its supporting clauses are this estate's own; it is **not cleared**.
- **Two rules claimed a field their own evidence did not cite.** IOS-4 claimed land administration and IOS-2 health informatics with no such source on the rule. Both citations are now on the rules.
- **IOS-1 restated EM-15 without citing it**, which is a breach of this document's own P1 by the document that states P1. The citation is now on the rule and the rule is narrowed to what EM-15 does not say.
- **`bitemporal` does not exist in `SDD-09` v1.1.** §10 named it as a landing site; it is a later-schema construct added without a version bump of that specification, and it is now a third platform demand rather than a landing site.
- **Three internal quotations had drifted**, one of them an ellipsis that removed the clause conceding what the outline *had* covered. All are restored verbatim.

**What the pass could not check.** It did not open a single one of the outside sources either. **No external clause number or quotation in this document has been independently verified by anybody**, and §9.1's third qualification is the whole of what is known about them.

**And the pass was run by an agent reading the same files as the agent that wrote them.** It is not step 15, and it is not a person.

### 9.4 The step this standard has not taken

**Step 15 of the house method: hand it to somebody who has never seen the subject.** It has not been done. Nobody unfamiliar with identity, ownership or state has been given this document and asked to describe a record from it, and every difference between what such a reader produced and what the register already holds would be a fault in this standard until somebody showed otherwise. **It is the only step that tests what this document claims, nothing else depends on it, and it is therefore the step most likely to be skipped.** This standard came into force before it was done, by the owner's ruling of 29 September 2026, and the step remains to be taken.

## 11. Conformance

**A claim of conformance to this standard is made rule by rule, or it is not a claim.**

To claim conformance for a register is to state, for each of IOS-1 to IOS-47, that the register satisfies it — and where it does not, to have recorded a finding with a named owner and a date. **A finding with an owner does not defeat the claim. A failure that was never recorded does.** A clause added to a rule in version 0.3 is claimed with the rule it joined, as part of it; the fifteen rules of version 0.3 are claimed on the same terms as the thirty-two before them, and on no others.

Three things follow, and they are the whole of the claim's discipline:

1. **A count of findings of zero is a bad sign, not a good one.** The house method's position is that a run of work ending with no refusals has almost certainly lowered the bar rather than met it, and the method volume says the same of findings against the groundwork.
2. **No conformance claim may cite a check result.** Not one of the forty-two checks in §6.1 exists. A claim that says *the checks pass* is a claim about a program nobody has written.
3. **A conformance claim made before step 15 says only that the document was followed**, not that the document works. That distinction is the reason §9.4 exists.

## 12. What this standard does not claim about itself

1. **It does not claim to have been tested by use.** It came into force by the owner's ruling of 29 September 2026, its rules as version 0.4 stated them.
2. **It does not claim that forty-seven is the right number of rules**, or that the eight families are the right cut. The first nineteen came from a design that had not taken stock when it was written; the next thirteen came from a stocktake taken in one sitting on the day they were drafted; the last fifteen were written from two answers settled earlier in their own week, each of which had swept its obligations, and from a check that placed every obligation against the standards as they then stood.
3. **It does not report a result from any check.** Forty-two are printed and none is built.
4. **It does not claim to be the right length.** It is above the band its own estate's standards occupy, and §9.3 says by how much and what would come out.
5. **It does not claim its counts are complete.** Each is stated with the file it was made on and the moment it was made; the registers are regenerated views, and a count made on 31 August 2026 is a count of that day's view. **One count of the design it was drawn from did not reproduce and is reported at F-GW01-3 rather than smoothed.**
6. **It does not claim to have read its outside sources in the original.** §9.1.
7. **It does not claim the boundary against `MDM-001` is in the right place.** It is drawn here for the first time, by this pass, and it is the first thing a reviewer who knows that standard should attack.
8. **It has not been read by anybody who has never seen this subject.** §9.4.
9. **It was drafted by an agent**, and the verification pass over it was run by the same agent reading the same files.
10. **It does not claim that the four catalogues belong here.** The first three are here by a ruling, the ruling records the case against itself, and the title of this document was changed to keep the cover true rather than because the shape was found to be right. The fourth is here by two approaches and one check that tested the alternative and did not take it, and §1.1 records the ground on which a reader reverses that.
11. **It does not claim that thirteen rules are enough for three subjects, or twelve for the fourth.** Four or five rules for a subject is what one sitting produced for the first three; the twelve for settings were tested against one catalogue of eight settings and no other. The design behind families A to D took a stocktake first; families E to G did not have one; family H had a stocktake, a sweep and a check, and has still been read by nobody who had never seen the subject.
